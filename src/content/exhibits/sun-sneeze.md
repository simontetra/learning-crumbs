Walk out of a dark room into bright sunshine. Do you sneeze? If so, you are not alone. Somewhere between about **10 and 35 percent** of people do.

People have noticed it for a very long time. The Greek thinker **Aristotle** wrote about it more than **2,300 years ago**.

It seems to run in families: if one of your parents has it, there is a good chance you will too. So scientists gave it a proper medical name, and they had some fun with it: **Autosomal Dominant Compelling Helio-Ophthalmic Outburst syndrome**. Take the first letters and you get **ACHOO**.

Nobody knows exactly how it works. The most popular idea is that the nerve that carries messages from your **eyes** runs very close to the nerve that feels things in your **nose and face**. When a sudden burst of bright light hits your eyes, some of the signal may spill over, and your nose thinks it has been tickled.

> **Look closer:** A name built so its first letters spell a word is called a *backronym*. Scientists clearly chose the long name to fit the short one.
