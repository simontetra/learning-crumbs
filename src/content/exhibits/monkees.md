In 1965 two television producers, **Bob Rafelson** and **Bert Schneider**, had an idea inspired by the Beatles' film *A Hard Day's Night*: a comedy series about a young pop group trying to make it. They needed a band, so they built one. Their advert in the Hollywood trade papers asked for "Folk & Roll Musicians" for "4 insane boys, age 17–21". Hundreds tried out. The four chosen were **Davy Jones**, **Micky Dolenz**, **Peter Tork** and **Michael Nesmith**.

The band also needed a name. Pop groups of the day had a habit of naming themselves after animals: the Beatles, the Animals, the Byrds, the Turtles. In his 1987 memoir, Davy Jones remembered someone suggesting **the Parrots**. He wasn't keen on the idea of a parrot on his shoulder. Then someone said **monkeys**, and everyone knew they had it.

Except it wasn't spelled *monkeys*. Just as the Beatles had swapped *beetles* for a word with *beat* in it, and the Byrds had dropped the *i* from *birds*, the new band became **the Monkees**. Accounts differ on exactly who chose what, as they often do when a lot of people are in the room. *The Monkees* first appeared on NBC on 12 September 1966.

There is another name in this story. In London, a young singer called **David Jones** was trying to get noticed. In 1965 he was advised to change his name, because a different Davy Jones, the one who would soon be a Monkee, was already well known from the stage musical *Oliver!* So the London singer picked a new surname, partly after Jim Bowie, an American frontiersman. He became **David Bowie**.

> **Look closer:** Michael Nesmith's mother was Bette Nesmith, the Dallas secretary who invented Liquid Paper. As a teenager he helped fill the bottles.
