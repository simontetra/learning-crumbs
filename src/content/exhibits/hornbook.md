Before children had books of their own, many learned their letters from a **hornbook**. It was not really a book at all. It was a flat wooden paddle, often with a handle, with a sheet of paper or vellum on the front. The sheet carried the alphabet, numbers and sometimes a short prayer.

A thin, see-through layer of **horn** was fixed over the sheet to protect it, and brass strips held the horn in place. The child held the paddle up and read the lesson through the horn. Some hornbooks used a mineral called mica instead.

Hornbooks were used in England from the mid-1400s until the late 1700s.

> **Look closer:** Many hornbooks had a hole in the handle for a cord or ribbon, so a child could carry the lesson around.
