Next to every hair follicle there is a **tiny muscle**. When you get cold, or scared, or a piece of music sends a shiver down your back, a nerve tells those muscles to tighten. Each one pulls its hair upright, and the skin around it bunches into a little bump.

For an animal with **thick fur**, this is very useful. Standing every hair on end fluffs up the coat and traps a layer of warm air next to the skin, like a duvet. A cat that is frightened puffs up its fur too.

We still have the same muscles, but very little fur. So when they tighten, all we get is a field of little bumps that look like the skin of a plucked goose, which is how they got their name.

In **2020**, scientists at **Harvard** found that the system is cleverer than it looks. In mice, the same nerve that causes goosebumps also wakes up the cells that grow new hair, when the cold goes on for a long time. Goosebumps are a quick fix; a thicker coat is the long-term plan.

> **Look closer:** The muscle that makes goosebumps is tiny. And the word *muscle* itself comes from the Latin for "little mouse".
