**Emil Jellinek** was a wealthy businessman who lived in Nice, in the south of France. He loved fast cars, raced them, and sold **Daimler** cars to his rich friends.

When he raced, he didn't use his own name. He entered under a nickname: **Mercedes**, the first name of his daughter, **Mercédès**, born in 1889. It's a Spanish name meaning **mercies**.

In April 1900, Jellinek ordered **36 cars** from the Daimler company. The new car they built for him, faster, lower and more modern than before, carried his daughter's name. The **Mercedes 35 hp**, designed by **Wilhelm Maybach**, was delivered just before Christmas **1900**. Many people call it the first truly modern car.

It was such a success that in **1902** the company registered **Mercedes** as its brand name. Years later, when Daimler joined forces with Karl Benz's company, the cars became **Mercedes-Benz**: a little girl's name next to the name of the man who patented the car.

> **Look closer:** Mercédès Jellinek grew up to be a singer. In all her life, she never owned a car.
