Earth's outer shell is not one solid piece. It is made of several large, thin and fairly rigid **plates** that move relative to one another. The places where they meet are where many earthquakes and volcanoes happen.

The movement is slow. The US Geological Survey says that tectonic plates move **roughly at the same rate that your fingernails grow**. Nobody can see it happen, but satellite GPS can measure the motion to within a fraction of a millimetre a year.

Slow movement adds up. Over millions of years, the plates have carried continents apart and pushed up mountain ranges.

> **Look closer:** The ground under your feet is moving right now, at about the speed of a fingernail.
