Charades probably began in France during the 18th century, as a kind of riddle. Players guessed a word by solving each of its syllables, with each one described separately.

The acted version became the most popular. The novelist William Makepeace Thackeray described it in *Vanity Fair* in 1848, which helped it spread.

In the 1930s and 1940s, and again after the Second World War, a changed version called "the Game" became popular in the United States, especially at parties.

> **Look closer:** The silent acting-out is the part that has lasted. Almost everyone who has played it has a favourite word they could never get across.
