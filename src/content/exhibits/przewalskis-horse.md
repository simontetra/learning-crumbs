**Przewalski's horse** (say it *sheh-VAL-skee*) is a stocky, sand-coloured horse with a short, upright mane, like a living cave painting. It is named after **Nikolai Przhevalsky**, a Russian explorer who brought news of it to European scientists in the late 1800s. In Mongolia it has its own name: **takhi**.

The horses lived on the grasslands of Mongolia and northern China, but hunting, competition with farm animals and harsh winters took their toll. The last one was seen in the wild in **1969**.

A few had been caught decades earlier for zoos and private collections. Every Przewalski's horse alive today descends from just **12** of those wild-caught horses, plus possibly a few domestic ones. Zoos around the world kept careful family records so the horses wouldn't become too inbred.

In **1992** the first horses were flown back to Mongolia, to **Hustai National Park** near the capital and to **Takhin Tal** in the Gobi. Their numbers have grown to hundreds in the wild, and in 2011 the species was moved from "extinct in the wild" to "endangered".

> **Look closer:** Their homeland lay along old Silk Road routes, the same trade paths that carried the secret of papermaking west from China.
