The **black robin** is a small, sooty bird that lives only on the Chatham Islands, far out in the Pacific east of New Zealand. Cats and rats had wiped it out almost everywhere, and by **1980** there were just **five** left in the world.

Only one of them was a female who could still lay eggs. Wildlife workers called her **Old Blue**, after the coloured band on her leg.

A New Zealand conservationist, **Don Merton**, and his team came up with a clever trick. Each time Old Blue laid her eggs, they quietly took them away and placed them in the nest of a different bird, the **Chatham Island tomtit**, which raised the chicks as its own. Old Blue, finding her nest empty, would lay again. That way she produced far more chicks than she could ever have raised herself.

Old Blue lived for more than 14 years, very old for a small robin. Today there are a few hundred black robins, and **every single one is descended from her**. It is about as narrow an escape as any species has had.

> **Look closer:** With every bird descended from one mother, scientists feared inbreeding would doom the species. So far it hasn't, perhaps because the robins had already squeezed through small numbers in their past.
