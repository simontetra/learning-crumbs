A water molecule has one oxygen atom and two hydrogen atoms. In ice, the molecules link to their neighbours in a regular, repeating pattern, and that pattern is **hexagonal**: six-sided.

A snowflake starts as a tiny crystal of ice that forms around a speck of dust in a cloud. That first crystal has six-fold symmetry, and it has to keep that symmetry as it grows. Water vapour attaches to its six points, and each arm grows out from there.

No two snowflakes are exactly alike, because the temperature and humidity change as each one falls. Each arm meets slightly different conditions, so the shapes differ.

> **Look closer:** The snowflake does not choose its six sides. They come from the way water molecules fit together as ice.
