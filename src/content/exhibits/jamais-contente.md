Electric cars sound like something new. They aren't. Around the year 1900, electric cars were a common sight, and for a while they were the fastest cars on Earth.

In France, two men were locked in a race to be the fastest. One was a nobleman, **Count Gaston de Chasseloup-Laubat**. The other was a Belgian engineer, **Camille Jenatzy**. Both drove electric cars, and they kept taking the record from each other.

Jenatzy decided to build a car for one thing only: speed. He gave it a body shaped like a **torpedo**, made of a light metal mixture. He sat on top, his head and shoulders sticking out into the wind. He named it **La Jamais Contente**: *Never Satisfied*.

On **29 April 1899**, near Paris, Jenatzy reached **105.88 km/h**, about 66 miles an hour. It was the first time a road vehicle had ever gone faster than **100 kilometres an hour**, and it did it on batteries.

Petrol cars soon took over, because they could go much further before needing more fuel. But a hundred and more years later, electric cars are back.

> **Look closer:** The name suits a speed-record car perfectly. However fast you go, someone will always want to go faster.
