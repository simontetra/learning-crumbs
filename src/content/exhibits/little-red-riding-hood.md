A girl walks through the woods to take food to her sick grandmother. On the way she meets a wolf, who runs ahead to the grandmother's house, and waits in her bed.

In **Charles Perrault's** version, printed in **1697**, the wolf eats the girl, and that is the end. There is no rescue. Perrault adds a warning: the most dangerous wolves are the friendly, gentle-seeming ones.

In the **Brothers Grimm** version, published in **1812**, a huntsman hears snoring, finds the wolf and cuts it open. The girl and her grandmother climb out unharmed. Then they fill the wolf's belly with stones. When it wakes and goes to the well for a drink, it falls in and drowns.

> **Look closer:** The red hood is Perrault's addition. It is the detail that gave the story its name.
