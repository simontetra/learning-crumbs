The **Arctic tern** is a slim white seabird with a black cap and a sharp red beak. It doesn't look like a champion. But no animal on Earth makes a longer journey every year.

Arctic terns raise their chicks in the far north, in places like **Greenland** and **Iceland**, during the Arctic summer. Then they fly all the way to the seas around **Antarctica**, at the other end of the world, arriving for the southern summer. Then they fly back.

For a long time nobody knew exactly which way they went. Then scientists fitted terns with tiny tracking devices called **geolocators**, each weighing about **1.4 grams**, less than a paperclip. In **2010** they reported what they'd found. The birds didn't fly straight: they zigzagged down the Atlantic, following winds and stopping where there was food. On average, each tern flew about **70,900 kilometres** in a year.

Terns can live for many years. Add it all up, and in a lifetime a single Arctic tern may fly the distance of about **three round trips to the Moon**.

> **Look closer:** Because it flies from one summer to the other, an Arctic tern may see more daylight than any other animal on Earth.
