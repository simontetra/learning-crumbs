Early cars were toys for the rich, built slowly by hand. **Henry Ford** wanted to change that. He wanted to build a car that ordinary working families could afford.

On **1 October 1908** he launched the **Model T**, a simple, sturdy "everyman's car". It sold well, but Ford wanted to make it faster and cheaper still.

In **1913**, at his factory in **Highland Park**, Detroit, he tried something new. Instead of workers walking around a car, the car came to them. Each chassis was pulled slowly along a **moving assembly line**, and each worker did one job as it passed. Building a chassis had taken **12 hours and 8 minutes**. By 1914 it took **93 minutes**.

The price tumbled, from **$850** in 1909 to **$260** by 1924. More than **15 million** Model Ts were built before production ended in 1927.

> **Look closer:** At first Model Ts came in different colours. Later Ford stuck to **black**, which dried faster, giving rise to the famous saying: *you can have any colour you like, as long as it's black*.
