**Beatrix Potter** loved drawing animals, especially her own pets, and she drew them carefully and truthfully.

In September 1893, while away from home, she heard that **Noel Moore**, the five-year-old son of her former governess, was ill in bed with scarlet fever. To cheer him up, she sent him a letter. It began: "My dear Noel, I don't know what to write to you, so I shall tell you a story". The story was about four little rabbits, and she drew pictures all the way through it, in ink, starring a naughty one named after her own pet rabbit, Peter.

Years later she turned the letter into a little book. At least six publishers said no. They wanted a bigger, more expensive book with colour pictures; she wanted a small, cheap one that a child could hold. So in **1901** she paid to print **250 copies** herself.

Then one publisher, Frederick Warne, changed its mind, on condition that she coloured the pictures. *The Tale of Peter Rabbit* came out in **October 1902**. The first printing was **8,000** copies, and it has never been out of print since.

> **Look closer:** Beatrix Potter spent much of the money from her books buying farms in the Lake District, the same countryside where pencils began. When she died in 1943 she left about 4,000 acres of it to the National Trust, so it would be looked after for good.
