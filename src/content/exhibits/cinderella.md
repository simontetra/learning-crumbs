A girl is treated badly by her stepmother and stepsisters, and is left at home to clean the ashes from the fire. With help from a fairy godmother, she goes to the palace ball. When the clock strikes midnight, she runs away and leaves a slipper behind on the stairs.

The slipper is the story's most famous object. In **Charles Perrault's** version, published in **1697**, it is a *pantoufle de verre*, a slipper of glass. Some scholars think Perrault mixed up the French words for glass (*verre*) and fur (*vair*), and that the slipper was first made of fur. Most now treat the glass as a deliberate, magical choice.

In the **Brothers Grimm** version of **1812**, the slipper is gold.

> **Look closer:** A version of the story was written down in China around 860, more than 800 years before Perrault. It tells of a girl called Ye Xian, who loses a golden slipper.
