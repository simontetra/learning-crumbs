The stories we call the Arabian Nights were gathered over many centuries, from Persia, India and the Arab world. Their frame is the story of Scheherazade, who has to tell King Shahryar a new tale every night, stopping at dawn so that he keeps her alive. An Arabic collection of the tales dates to about 850.

Two of the best-known stories, Aladdin and Ali Baba, were not in those early collections at all. The French scholar Antoine Galland added them when he published his translation between 1704 and 1717. He said he had heard Aladdin from a Syrian storyteller from Aleppo. Much of what Europe thinks of as the Arabian Nights comes from his book.

> **Look closer:** A lamp that a boy rubs in one story may have come from a storyteller in Aleppo, passed through a French translator, and landed on a shelf in England.
