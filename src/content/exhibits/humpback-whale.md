For about 150 years, whales were hunted for oil to burn in lamps, grease machines and make soap and margarine. Between the late 1700s and the mid-1900s, whalers killed at least **300,000 humpback whales** around the world.

In the South Atlantic, where humpbacks gather off Brazil to have their calves, numbers fell to about **440** by 1958. It looked like the end.

People's feelings about whales began to change in the 1960s and 70s, partly because of the whales' own voices. In **1986** the International Whaling Commission's ban on commercial whaling of all whales came into force.

The humpbacks responded. A 2019 study estimated that the South Atlantic population had grown back to nearly **25,000**, about **93 per cent** of what it was before whaling began. Humpbacks in many other oceans have recovered too, though whales still face dangers from ships, fishing nets and noise.

> **Look closer:** In 1970 a recording of humpback whale song became a surprise best-selling album, and it helped turn people against whaling. Humpback song was later included on the golden records carried into space by the Voyager probes.
