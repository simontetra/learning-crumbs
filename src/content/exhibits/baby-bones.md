Here is something surprising: when you were born, you had **more bones** than you have now.

A newborn baby has somewhere around **275 to 300** bones. A grown-up has about **206**. So where do the others go?

They don't disappear. Many of a baby's bones start out as **cartilage**, a tough but bendy material, like the stuff in the tip of your nose and your ears. That bendiness helps a baby fit inside its mother and squeeze out into the world. As the child grows, the cartilage slowly turns into hard bone, and many separate pieces **join together** into one. This carries on until the teenage years or a little after.

You can feel one example on a baby's head. The **skull** is made of several plates with a soft gap between them on top, called the **fontanelle**, or soft spot. It lets the head change shape a little at birth, and closes up within about a year or two.

> **Look closer:** Your own skull is made of plates that joined together when you were small. Run a finger over your head and you can't feel the joins any more.
