Insomnia is when you regularly have problems sleeping. The usual signs are:

- finding it hard to fall asleep
- waking several times in the night
- waking early and being unable to get back to sleep
- still feeling tired after waking
- feeling tired, irritable or unable to concentrate during the day

Insomnia that lasts less than three months is short-term. If it lasts three months or longer, it is long-term. The NHS says it usually improves once a person changes their sleep habits.

Common causes include stress, anxiety or depression, noise, a room that is too hot or too cold, alcohol, caffeine and nicotine, and shift work or jet lag. Other conditions can also disturb sleep, including long-term pain, sleep apnoea and restless legs syndrome.

**How many people have it?** The American Academy of Sleep Medicine's figures for adults in the United States are:

- **Occasional symptoms:** 30 to 35 per cent
- **Short-term insomnia:** 15 to 20 per cent
- **Chronic insomnia disorder** (at least three nights a week for at least three months): about 10 per cent

A separate survey of US adults found that about 12 per cent reported having been diagnosed with chronic insomnia. The two figures measure different things, so they are not the same.

> **Look closer:** Most people have a bad night now and then. Insomnia is when the bad nights keep coming, and they begin to affect the day.
