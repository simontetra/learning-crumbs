An igloo is a snow house. The Inuktitut word *iglu* means house, and Inuit in parts of the Arctic built igloos as winter shelters. Builders used snow that the wind had packed hard, which is easier to cut into blocks than soft, settled snow.

- **Blocks:** the snow was cut into large blocks with saws and blade-like tools, then shaped to fit together.
- **Dome:** the blocks were laid in a spiral, leaning inward until they closed into a dome. Gaps were filled with snow to keep the warmth in.
- **Uses:** the smallest igloos were temporary shelters, often for a night or two on hunting trips. Larger ones were family homes, and the largest held community feasts and dances.

> **Look closer:** Many igloos were used for only one or two nights. The house was built from the snow that was already there.
