The atmosphere is made of layers, each with its own temperature and job.

1. **Troposphere** (ground to about 12 km). The densest layer. It holds most of the air and the water vapour, and all the weather, clouds and aircraft flights happen here.
2. **Stratosphere** (about 12 to 50 km). It contains the **ozone layer**, which absorbs harmful ultraviolet light. The temperature rises with height here.
3. **Mesosphere** (about 50 to 80 km). It gets colder with height, and its top is the coldest place in the Earth system. Most meteors burn up here.
4. **Thermosphere** (about 80 to 700 km). The air is very sparse, and temperatures rise with height. The International Space Station orbits here, and auroras sometimes appear.
5. **Exosphere** (about 700 to 10,000 km). The outermost layer, so thin that particles can escape into space. Most Earth satellites orbit here.

The layers do not have sharp edges. The boundaries are approximate, and the top of the exosphere is not agreed.

> **Look closer:** The weather you feel happens in the bottom 12 kilometres, a tiny slice of the whole. Everything above it is far thinner.
