This room is mostly about animals, but here is a place that came back from the brink.

For centuries London poured its sewage and factory waste straight into the **Thames**. In hot summers the river could smell terrible, and in places the water held so little oxygen that fish couldn't survive. In **1957** scientists at London's **Natural History Museum** declared large parts of it **"biologically dead"**.

Slowly, things changed. Better sewage treatment and cleaner industry let oxygen back into the water, and wildlife began to return.

In 2021 the Zoological Society of London published its first full health check of the tidal Thames. It found **seals**, **seahorses**, **eels** and even small **sharks**, such as the smooth-hound. Scientists have recorded **115** kinds of fish in the river.

It is not a perfect ending. London's Victorian sewers still overflow into the river after heavy rain. A huge new tunnel, the **Thames Tideway Tunnel**, has been built to catch most of those spills. Rising temperatures and sea levels are new worries.

> **Look closer:** Seal pups are now born in the Thames estuary every year. Not bad for a river once declared dead.
