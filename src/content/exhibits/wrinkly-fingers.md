Stay in the bath long enough and your fingertips go wrinkly, like little prunes. For a long time people assumed the skin was simply soaking up water and swelling.

But there was a clue that something else was going on. The wrinkles are made by the **nervous system**: tiny blood vessels under the skin tighten, and the skin above them puckers. Your body is doing it on purpose.

So why would it? In **2013**, scientists at **Newcastle University** tried an experiment. They asked people to pick up marbles and move them from one box to another, once with ordinary fingers and once after soaking their hands in warm water for half an hour. With **wet** marbles, the wrinkly fingers were **12% faster**. With dry marbles, the wrinkles made no difference.

The team compared the wrinkles to the **tread on a car tyre**, which channels water away so the tyre can grip a wet road. Perhaps wrinkly fingers helped our ancestors pick up food from wet plants or keep their footing in the rain.

> **Look closer:** Not every scientist is convinced yet; some experiments haven't found the same grip effect. That's how science works: one experiment is a clue, not the final answer.
