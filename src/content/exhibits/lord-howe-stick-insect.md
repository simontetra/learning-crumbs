The **Lord Howe Island stick insect** is a shiny black insect as long as your hand, up to 20 centimetres. It is so big and chunky that local people called it the **tree lobster**.

It lived only on Lord Howe Island, off the east coast of Australia. In **1918** a supply ship, the *Makambo*, ran aground there, and black rats came ashore. The rats ate the stick insects, and after **1920** none were seen. The tree lobster was declared extinct.

More than 20 kilometres away stands **Ball's Pyramid**, a jagged spike of rock rising straight out of the sea, all that's left of an ancient volcano. Climbers had reported seeing dead stick insects there. In **2001** a team of scientists climbed up, searched by day, found nothing, and went back after dark. Under a single scraggly shrub they found **24** tree lobsters, hidden for some 80 years.

In 2003 two breeding pairs were collected. **Melbourne Zoo** bred them so well that by 2012 it had raised more than 9,000. A plan to clear the rats from Lord Howe Island was approved in 2018, so that one day the tree lobsters can go home.

> **Look closer:** Things thought lost do sometimes turn up. Some pages of Gutenberg Bibles survive only because bookbinders reused them as scrap inside the covers of other books.
