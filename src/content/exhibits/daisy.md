Look at a lawn early in the morning and the little daisies are shut tight. As the sun comes up, they open their white petals around a yellow middle. In the evening, they close again.

People more than a thousand years ago noticed this too. In **Old English**, the language spoken in England before the Normans arrived, the flower was called *dæges ēage*, the **day's eye**. It opens its eye when the day begins and closes it at night.

Over the centuries the words ran together. In the Middle Ages it was spelled *dayeseye*. Say that quickly a few times and you have **daisy**.

> **Look closer:** "Fresh as a daisy" makes sense now. A daisy greets every morning as if it has just woken up, because, in a way, it has.
