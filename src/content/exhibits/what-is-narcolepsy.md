Narcolepsy is a rare, long-term brain disorder. It means the brain cannot control sleeping and waking normally, so people fall asleep suddenly and at the wrong times.

The main symptoms are:

- **Excessive daytime sleepiness:** difficulty staying awake, sudden sleep attacks and trouble concentrating.
- **Cataplexy:** a sudden loss of muscle control that can make a person fall. Strong emotions, such as laughter or anger, can trigger it.
- **Sleep paralysis:** being unable to move or speak when falling asleep or waking.
- **Vivid dreams and night waking:** dreams that seem intense or real, and waking often at night.

The cause is often unclear. Many cases involve a shortage of hypocretin, a brain chemical that helps keep us awake. Treatment includes planned daytime naps, a regular bedtime routine and medicines. There is no cure, but these can ease the symptoms.

> **Look closer:** The sleepiness in narcolepsy comes on suddenly, which is different from the tiredness of a short night.
