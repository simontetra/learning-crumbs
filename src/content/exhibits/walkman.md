Before 1979, if you wanted music, you mostly listened at home, or carried a big, heavy radio.

**Masaru Ibuka**, one of the founders of the Japanese company **Sony**, flew a lot and wanted music on the long journeys. Engineers adapted a small Sony tape recorder for him. His fellow founder **Akio Morita** saw what it could be and pushed the team to make a little player anyone could buy.

The **Walkman** went on sale in Japan on **1 July 1979**. It played cassettes through light headphones, and it was small enough to clip on a belt. Sony expected to sell about 5,000 a month. More than **30,000** sold in the first two months.

The first Walkman had **two headphone sockets**, so two people could listen together. It also had a **"hotline" button**: press it, and a tiny microphone mixed your voice into the music, so you could talk to your friend without taking your headphones off.

> **Look closer:** At first the Walkman had different names in different countries: the **Soundabout** in America, the **Stowaway** in Britain and the **Freestyle** in Sweden. In the end, Walkman won everywhere.
