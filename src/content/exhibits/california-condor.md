The **California condor** is the largest flying bird in North America, with wings that stretch more than nine feet from tip to tip. It glides for hours over canyons, looking for the dead animals it eats.

By the 1980s it was dying out fast, and the main cause was a surprise: **lead**. Condors were eating carcasses left by hunters, swallowing fragments of lead bullets, and slowly being poisoned.

Biologists made a hard and controversial decision: catch them all. On **19 April 1987** they captured the last free-flying condor, a male known as **AC-9**. Now there were **27** California condors in the world, and none of them in the sky.

Zoos in San Diego and Los Angeles bred them carefully, and in **1992** the first young condors were released back into the wild. By the end of 2025 there were **607** condors, with **392** of them flying free in California, Arizona, Utah and Mexico.

Lead is still their biggest danger, so many hunters in condor country now use lead-free bullets.

> **Look closer:** AC-9 was released back into the wild in 2002, fifteen years after he was caught, and flew free again over the hills he came from.
