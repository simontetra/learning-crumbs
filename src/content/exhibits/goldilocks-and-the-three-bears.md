Three bears live in a house in the woods: a great big Daddy Bear, a middle-sized Mummy Bear, and a little Baby Bear. While they are out walking, an intruder lets herself in and tries their porridge, their chairs and their beds.

The first version of this story did not have a girl at all. Robert Southey published "The Story of the Three Bears" in 1837, in a collection called *The Doctor*. The intruder was an **old woman**.

Over the next century, the intruder grew younger. She became a little girl with golden hair, and in about 1904 she was given the name we know today.

> **Look closer:** The bears' porridge is too hot, then too cold, then just right. The bowls, chairs and beds do the same, which is why it is a story about trying things until one fits.
