In 1829 the new **Liverpool and Manchester Railway** was nearly finished, but its owners hadn't decided how to pull the trains. Horses? Stationary engines hauling ropes? Or the new steam **locomotives**?

They held a contest. In **October 1829**, at **Rainhill** near Liverpool, engines had to run up and down a level stretch of track again and again, with a **£500** prize for the winner.

There were five entries. The crowd's favourite, **Novelty**, raced along at 28 miles an hour, then broke a pipe. **Sans Pareil** cracked a cylinder. **Perseverance** was damaged on the way there. And **Cycloped** wasn't a steam engine at all: it was powered by a **horse** walking on a moving belt, and it was withdrawn after an accident.

Only one engine finished: the **Rocket**, designed by **George and Robert Stephenson**. Pulling 13 tons, it reached **30 miles an hour**, astonishing for its day. The railway opened in 1830 with Stephenson engines, and its success set off the great railway age.

> **Look closer:** The opening day, 15 September 1830, was also the scene of the first widely reported railway death. A politician, William Huskisson, stepped onto the track to greet the Duke of Wellington and was struck by the Rocket.
