The Royal Game of Ur is a race game for two players. Each player moves seven pieces around a board of twenty squares and tries to bear them off before the other player does. The game is played with four-sided dice.

Leonard Woolley excavated the Royal Cemetery at Ur between 1922 and 1934, and found boards of the game there. The game was played across the Middle East until late antiquity.

The rules survive on a clay tablet, written around 177 BC by a scribe named Itti-Marduk-balāṭu. It was found in Babylon in 1880 and is now in the British Museum. Irving Finkel translated it in the early 1980s.

> **Look closer:** The rules are old enough to be read today because someone wrote them down. A game that was passed on by players alone would probably have been forgotten long ago.
