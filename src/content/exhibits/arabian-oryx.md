The **Arabian oryx** is a white antelope with long, straight horns, built to survive the deserts of Arabia. For centuries it was hunted, but once hunters had cars and rifles, its numbers crashed. By **1972** it was **extinct in the wild**.

Luckily, a rescue had already begun. In the early 1960s, **Operation Oryx** caught a few of the last wild animals, and others were donated, including some from the King of Saudi Arabia. They were sent to **Phoenix Zoo in Arizona**, whose hot, dry climate suited them. This "World Herd" started with just nine animals and grew year after year.

On **31 January 1982**, oryx bred in America were released into the desert of **Oman**. More releases followed in Saudi Arabia, Israel and the United Arab Emirates. In **2011** the oryx became the **first animal ever** to be moved from "extinct in the wild" to "vulnerable" on the international Red List. Today there are more than a thousand in the wild.

There was a setback along the way. Poaching in Oman's sanctuary was so bad that in **2007** it became the first place ever removed from UNESCO's World Heritage list.

> **Look closer:** Seen side-on, an oryx's two straight horns can line up to look like one. It's a popular idea that travellers' tales of oryx helped inspire the legend of the unicorn, though nobody can prove it.
