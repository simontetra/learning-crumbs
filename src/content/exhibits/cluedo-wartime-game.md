Cluedo was thought up during the Second World War, when families spent long evenings indoors. Anthony Pratt, a British designer, worked on it while sheltering from air raids in Birmingham.

He drew on murder-mystery party games and the detective stories of the time. His patent was filed in 1944 and granted in 1947. The game was first made in Britain in 1949 by Waddingtons, which named it **Cluedo**, blending "clue" with the older game **Ludo**. Parker Brothers sold it in the United States the same year under the shorter name **Clue**.

Pratt's first design was bigger than the game we know. It had ten characters, eleven rooms and nine weapons, including a bomb and a syringe. Some of these were dropped before it reached the shops.

> **Look closer:** In 1953 Pratt sold the international rights for £5,000, so he received nothing from the versions that went on to be hits abroad.
