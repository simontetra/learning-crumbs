Ice is clear because its water molecules form a regular crystal, and light passes through it with little scattering. Snow is different. It is made of many small ice crystals, with air gaps between them.

Each crystal has an irregular, complex shape, so light bounces off in many directions. Light of every colour is scattered in the same way, and the colours mix back together into white.

The Alaska Science Forum adds that the large grains in snow scatter all the visible colours about equally, so the snow keeps the colour of the light that falls on it.

> **Look closer:** Crush a piece of clear ice into powder and you get something white. The ice has not changed. Breaking it into many small pieces is enough to scatter the light.
