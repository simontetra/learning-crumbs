**Karl Benz** had built one of the world's first motor cars, the **Benz Patent-Motorwagen**, a spindly three-wheeler. But he wasn't good at selling it, and few people believed it was much use.

His wife **Bertha** decided to prove it was. Early on **5 August 1888**, without telling Karl, she set off with their sons **Eugen**, 15, and **Richard**, 13, to visit her mother in **Pforzheim**, about **106 kilometres** away. Nobody had ever driven a car that far.

The journey was an adventure. The car had no fuel tank, so when the petrol ran low she bought a cleaning fluid called ligroin from a **pharmacy** in Wiesloch, now remembered as the world's first filling station. She cleared a blocked fuel pipe with her **hatpin** and used her **garter** to insulate a wire. A blacksmith fixed the chain, and when the wooden brakes wore out, a cobbler fitted strips of **leather**, the first brake linings. On the uphill stretches, Richard steered while Bertha and Eugen pushed.

They reached her mother's house safely, and Karl, back home, sent a frantic telegram. The trip made headlines, brought the first sales, and Bertha suggested improvements, including an extra low gear for hills.

> **Look closer:** Her route is now the signposted Bertha Benz Memorial Route, which people drive in her honour.
