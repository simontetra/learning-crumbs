Most of the time the ground beneath us is still. But along some faults, rock on either side is held in place by friction until the strain becomes too great. Then it slips, and the shaking spreads out through the rock.

On **22 May 1960**, off the coast of southern Chile, the ground moved in what is recorded as the **largest earthquake of the twentieth century**. It is generally agreed to have had a **magnitude of 9.5**, although some studies have proposed 9.4 or 9.6.

The shaking did great damage along the coast. The death toll was first given as much higher, then scaled back to **1,655**. The figure was never fully resolved.
