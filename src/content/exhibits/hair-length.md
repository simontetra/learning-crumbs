Every hair on your body grows out of a tiny pit in the skin called a **follicle**. And every follicle works on a timer, going round and round through three stages.

First comes the **growing** stage. The hair gets longer, day after day. Then comes a short **pause**, when the hair stops growing and comes loose at the root. Then a **resting** stage, until the old hair falls out and a new one starts growing in its place.

The secret is in how long the growing stage lasts. On your **head**, it lasts a long time: **two to seven years**. So head hair can grow very long before its timer runs out. On your **arms and legs**, the growing stage lasts only **a few months**. By then the hair is still short, so it stops, rests and falls out, and a new short one takes its place.

That's why your arm hair always seems to stay the same length, even though you never cut it. It isn't that it stopped. It's that each hair only gets a few months, then hands over to the next.

> **Look closer:** It also explains eyelashes. Their timer is short too, which is lucky. Imagine trying to see through eyelashes as long as your hair.
