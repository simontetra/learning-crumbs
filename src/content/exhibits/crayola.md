**Binney & Smith**, a company run by two cousins, **Edwin Binney** and **C. Harold Smith**, made black pigments and other colours for industry. They also made chalk for schools, which put them in touch with classrooms, where children needed good, cheap wax crayons. In **1903** the company began making its own. The first box held **eight colours**: red, orange, yellow, green, blue, violet, brown and black. It cost a **nickel**, five cents.

The crayons needed a name. Edwin's wife, **Alice Binney**, had been a schoolteacher. She took the French word *craie*, meaning chalk, and added *ola*, from *oléagineux*, meaning oily. Oily chalk: **Crayola**.

The name has outlasted almost everything else the company made. Today there are well over a hundred Crayola colours, and the company itself took the name Crayola.

> **Look closer:** Like Pepsi and Amazon, Crayola is a made-up word. A good invented name can end up sounding as if it has always existed.
