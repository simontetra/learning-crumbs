When people in Tudor England parted, they might say **"God be with you"**, a little blessing for the journey home.

Said quickly, again and again, it got squashed. The oldest known written example is in a **1573** letter by the scholar **Gabriel Harvey**, who wrote it as **"godbwyes"**. You can almost hear someone hurrying it out of the door.

But how did *God* turn into *good*? Nobody can be completely sure, but the likeliest answer is that people were already saying **good day** and **good night**, so *good-bye* sounded like it belonged with them.

For a long time both the long and short versions were used side by side, until the early 1800s. Then *goodbye* won, and most people forgot it had ever been a blessing.

> **Look closer:** Gabriel Harvey wrote his letter with a quill. Long before email, a goodbye was something you could watch drying on the page.
