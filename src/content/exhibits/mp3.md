A song on a CD is a huge amount of data. In the 1980s and 1990s that was far too much to send down a telephone line or keep on a small computer.

A German engineer, **Karlheinz Brandenburg**, worked on the problem at the **Fraunhofer** research institute in **Erlangen**. His idea relied on how our ears work. When a loud sound plays, we can't hear quieter sounds close to it. So why store them? His system works out which parts of the music you wouldn't notice, and leaves them out. What's left is a much smaller file that sounds almost the same.

In **1988** he tested it on a song with nothing but a single voice: *Tom's Diner*, by the American singer **Suzanne Vega**. With no instruments to hide behind, every mistake showed. The result was full of scratchy distortion, so the team went back to work on it.

On **14 July 1995** they chose a short name for the files: **.mp3**, from "MPEG-1 Layer 3". MP3s let people carry thousands of songs on a tiny player, and send music across the internet.

> **Look closer:** Brandenburg was born in Erlangen, the same town where he and his team built the MP3.
