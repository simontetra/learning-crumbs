The end of the Permian period, about 252 million years ago, saw the worst extinction in the history of life on Earth. Scientists call it the **Great Dying**. It wiped out about **90 per cent of ocean species** and about 75 per cent of land species, according to the Smithsonian.

The leading explanation is **volcanism**. Huge eruptions in what is now Siberia, the Siberian Traps, released great amounts of magma and gas. Science News reports that the eruptions came in pulses, starting roughly 300,000 years before the extinction. They released carbon dioxide and methane, which warmed the land and sea. In the tropics, ocean temperatures rose by at least 10 degrees Celsius.

Researchers led by Justin Penn argue that the main killer may have been a loss of oxygen in the oceans, rather than the heat alone. This is still a theory under study.

> **Look closer:** The Great Dying built up over a long period, with eruptions in pulses, not in a single moment like the asteroid that hit Chicxulub.
