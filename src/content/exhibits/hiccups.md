Under your lungs is a big, dome-shaped muscle called the **diaphragm**. Every time you breathe in, it pulls down to draw air into your lungs.

A **hiccup** happens when the diaphragm suddenly **twitches** by itself. You gulp in a quick breath, and a split second later your **vocal cords** snap shut to stop it. That snap is the **"hic"**. Nobody is quite sure what hiccups are for, if anything.

Most hiccups stop after a few minutes. **Charles Osborne's** didn't. On **13 June 1922**, he was hanging up a heavy hog on his farm when he fell, and started hiccuping. A doctor later suspected he'd damaged a tiny part of his brain that controls breathing.

He hiccuped **20 to 40 times a minute**, every day, for **68 years**, an estimated **430 million** hiccups. Yet he lived a full life: he married twice, had eight children, and worked selling farm machinery and auctioning livestock. Then one day in **1990**, the hiccups simply stopped, and nobody knows why.

> **Look closer:** Over the years he learned to breathe in a way that muffled the "hic", so people he met often didn't notice at all.
