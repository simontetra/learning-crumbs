A ger, also called a yurt, is a round home. For generations, nomadic herders in Mongolia and Central Asia lived in gers, and they moved camp with their animals. Its walls are a lattice of wooden struts, and its roof poles rise from a central wooden ring.

- **Felt:** people layered sheep's wool, sprinkled it with water and worked it into thick mats. Several layers of felt kept the warmth in, and a white cloth could be added in cold or wet weather.
- **Portable:** a ger was easy to put up, light to carry and resistant to wind, which suited families who moved often.
- **Still moving:** modern yurts are also portable, and one model can be put up by two people in about an hour.

> **Look closer:** A ger was built to be taken down, carried and put up again. The house moved with the family.
