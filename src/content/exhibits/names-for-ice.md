You may have heard that the Inuit have hundreds of words for snow. That claim has been **exaggerated**. Early sources mentioned four or five snow words, and popular retellings inflated the number to 25, 50, 100 or more.

The real picture is more interesting. In West Greenlandic, there are two basic snow words: one for snow falling through the air, and one for snow lying on the ground. Yupik speakers distinguish at least 99 kinds of sea ice, according to the linguist K. David Harrison.

Counting words is harder than it sounds, because these languages build very long words from smaller parts. Count the roots, and the number is much smaller. English also has its own snow words, such as sleet, slush and flurry.

> **Look closer:** A language gives names to the things its speakers need to tell apart. Where snow and sea ice shape daily life, it makes sense to have words for them.
