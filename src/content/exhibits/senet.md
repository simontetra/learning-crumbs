Senet was played in ancient Egypt on a board of thirty squares, arranged in three rows of ten. Fragments of boards that may be senet were found in tombs from about 3100 BC, in the First Dynasty. The first clear painting of the game is in the tomb of Hesy-Re, from about 2620 BC.

Senet later became linked with the afterlife. Chapter 17 of the Book of the Dead connects the game with the journey after death.

> **Look closer:** Some of the earliest boards are only fragments, so the rules are not known for certain. The surviving paintings show a game that was played by ordinary people and kings alike.
