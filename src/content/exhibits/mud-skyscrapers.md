Shibam, in Yemen, is a town of mud-brick houses. Many of them rise five to eleven storeys, and their tall walls stand close together along the streets. The town is nicknamed the "Manhattan of the Desert", and also the "Chicago of the Desert".

- **Rebuilding:** the houses were rebuilt many times over the centuries. Most of what stands today dates to the 16th century, after a flood in about 1532 to 1533.
- **Protection:** UNESCO made Shibam a World Heritage Site in 1982, and it added the town to its list of sites in danger in 2015.

> **Look closer:** Shibam shows that tall buildings do not need steel. Earth, dried into bricks and built up carefully, can also reach many storeys.
