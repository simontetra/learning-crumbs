The sawing illusion was created by a British magician, Percy Thomas Tibbles, who performed as **P. T. Selbit**. He first performed it on 17 January 1921 at the Finsbury Park Empire in north London, with an assistant inside the box.

Horace Goldin, an American magician, claimed to have invented the trick, and most historians doubt it. He later took legal action against other magicians who performed it.

The illusion has been performed ever since. In 1949 it was broadcast live by the BBC, with a commentary by Brian Johnston, and in 1956 a version on BBC television caused such alarm that the switchboard was jammed with viewers. David Copperfield performed a version with a large saw in the 1980s.

> **Look closer:** The trick is about showmanship as much as cleverness. This room tells the history of the illusion and the people who performed it, and does not explain how it is done.
