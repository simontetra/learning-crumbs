The map on most classroom walls was made by Gerardus Mercator in 1569. He was not trying to show the land accurately. He was trying to help sailors.

A ship that holds one compass heading travels in a straight line, and Mercator's map shows that line as straight too. To make this work on a flat sheet, the map is stretched as you move away from the equator, and the stretch grows with every degree of latitude. The top and bottom of the map are pulled out far more than the middle.

At the poles the stretch becomes infinite, so the map cannot show them at all. Greenland, Russia and Canada look enormous as a result.

> **Look closer:** The map is not lying on purpose. It was built to answer one question, which was how to sail in a straight line, and every other question about size pays the price.
