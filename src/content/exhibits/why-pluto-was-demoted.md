For 76 years after its discovery, Pluto was counted as the ninth planet. In August 2006, the International Astronomical Union, the body that names and classifies objects in space, voted on a definition of a planet.

To be a planet, an object must meet three rules:

1. It **orbits the Sun**.
2. It is **massive enough to be round**, because its own gravity pulls it into a ball.
3. It has **cleared the neighbourhood** of its orbit, meaning it is the dominant body there and has swept up or flung away the other objects that share its path.

Pluto meets the first two. It fails the third, because it shares its region of space with many other icy bodies. So the Union reclassified it as a **dwarf planet**. That leaves eight planets in the Solar System.

> **Look closer:** Pluto did not change in 2006. The rule changed. Pluto is now the best-known member of a whole group of icy worlds beyond Neptune, which is why it became the example for that group.
