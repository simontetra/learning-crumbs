The Earth is huge compared with its air. Over **90 per cent** of the atmosphere lies within about **12 kilometres** of the surface. Earth's radius is about 6,370 kilometres, so that layer is about **0.2 per cent** of the radius.

The UCAR activity shrinks the Earth to the size of an apple. The atmosphere then becomes about as thick as the apple's skin. The New Zealand Ministry for the Environment makes the same comparison with a thin wax film on an apple.

> **Look closer:** A tennis ball is not a good comparison. Scale a tennis ball up to the size of the Earth, and the air layer where most of the air sits would be thinner than its fuzz. For a Swiss ball 65 centimetres across, that layer is about 0.6 millimetres thick, and the edge of space at 100 kilometres is about 5 millimetres out.
