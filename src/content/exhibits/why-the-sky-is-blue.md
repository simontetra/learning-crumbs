Sunlight looks white, but it is made of every colour, and each colour has its own wavelength. Blue has a short wavelength and red has a long one.

When sunlight meets the air, it hits tiny molecules, mainly nitrogen and oxygen. These molecules are much smaller than the wavelengths of visible light, and they scatter the short wavelengths far more than the long ones. This is called **Rayleigh scattering**. Blue light is scattered in all directions across the sky, so wherever you look overhead, blue reaches your eyes.

The sky is not violet, even though violet is scattered most. Our eyes are more sensitive to blue, and sunlight contains less violet to begin with.

> **Look closer:** The sky is blue because of the air itself, not because it reflects the sea. On a planet with no air, the sky would be black, even in daylight.
