The **bald eagle** has been the national bird of the United States since 1782. Yet within two centuries, Americans had nearly wiped it out.

Eagles were shot, and in some places bounties were paid for their bodies, because farmers and fishermen saw them as rivals. Then came a hidden danger. From the 1940s the insecticide **DDT** was sprayed widely on crops. It washed into rivers, built up in fish, and then in the eagles that ate them. It made their **eggshells so thin** that they cracked when the parents sat on them.

By **1963** only **417** nesting pairs were known in the lower 48 states. Rachel Carson's book *Silent Spring* (1962) had already warned the public about pesticides, and in **1972** the United States banned DDT. The eagle was protected by law, and its nests were guarded.

On **28 June 2007** the bald eagle was taken off the list of threatened and endangered species. By 2020 there were an estimated **316,700** bald eagles, including **71,400** nesting pairs.

> **Look closer:** Benjamin Franklin was not a fan. In a 1784 letter to his daughter, he called the bald eagle "a bird of bad moral character" and said the turkey was "a much more respectable bird".
