Scrabble was invented by an architect, Alfred Mosher Butts, in 1938, in Poughkeepsie, New York. It grew out of an earlier word game of his called Lexiko, and he first called it **Criss-Crosswords**.

The game was not a success at first. In 1948 James Brunot bought the manufacturing rights, renamed it **Scrabble** and agreed to pay Butts a royalty on every set sold. Brunot and his family made 2,400 sets in 1949 and lost money on them. In 1952 he licensed the making of the game to Selchow and Righter, which sold nearly four million sets in 1954.

Each English set has 100 tiles. There are 98 lettered tiles, worth between one and ten points, and two blank tiles worth nothing. Common letters such as L, N, R, S and T are worth one point, and the rare letters Q and Z are worth ten.

> **Look closer:** By 2008 about 150 million sets had been sold, in 121 countries and in more than 30 languages.
