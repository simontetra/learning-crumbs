Venus is closer to the Sun than Earth is, yet its surface is far hotter. It reaches about **900 °F (about 465 °C)**.

The reason is its **atmosphere**. It is thick and made mostly of carbon dioxide, with clouds of sulfuric acid. Sunlight gets in, but the heat cannot easily escape, so the surface keeps warming. This is the greenhouse effect, taken to an extreme.

So although Venus is in a region where water might once have been possible, its surface is far too hot for liquid water today.

> **Look closer:** Venus is the example of what happens when the warming goes too far. Earth's atmosphere also traps some heat, which keeps us warm, but on Venus the effect is so strong that the planet is roasting.
