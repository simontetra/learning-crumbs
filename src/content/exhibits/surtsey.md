On **14 November 1963**, an undersea volcano off the south coast of Iceland reached the surface of the sea, and kept erupting. The eruption almost certainly began a few days earlier, out of sight.

For nearly four years, lava and ash built up layer on layer. The island was named **Surtsey**, after Surtr, a fire giant in Norse myth. When the eruption ended on **5 June 1967**, the island covered about **2.7 square kilometres**.

Waves and weather have worn it down since, and it now covers about **1.3 square kilometres**. Surtsey is closed to visitors, so that scientists can watch what happens when life arrives on new land with no people to bring it.

> **Look closer:** Because the island was left alone, it has been used to study how plants and animals first settle on bare rock.
