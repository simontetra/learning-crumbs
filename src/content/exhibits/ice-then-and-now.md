NASA's GRACE satellites have measured Greenland's ice since 2002. They show it losing about **270 billion tonnes of ice a year**. The measurements have a gap between June 2017 and June 2018.

The Arctic sea ice season is also changing. A NASA study finds the melt season is about **15 days longer** than it was 30 years ago, growing by several days each decade.

Most of that change comes from the other end of the year. Melt starts a little earlier in spring, but the bigger factor is that the ice **freezes up later in autumn**. In the Beaufort and Chukchi Seas, freeze-up is arriving six to 11 days later each decade.

> **Look closer:** A longer melt season means the ice has less time to rebuild before the next summer. The sea ice is not simply melting in the summer. It is staying open for longer in the autumn.
