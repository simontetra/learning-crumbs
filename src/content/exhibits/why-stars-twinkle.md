Stars twinkle because their light has to pass through the atmosphere. The air is full of currents and layers of different temperature and density. These bend the starlight slightly, so it reaches our eyes along a jagged path, and the brightness seems to flicker.

Planets are closer, so they look like small discs rather than points. Light from the two edges of a disc bends in opposite directions, and the shifts cancel out, so the planet looks steady.

In space, above the atmosphere, stars do not twinkle at all. That is part of why the Hubble Space Telescope can produce such sharp images.

> **Look closer:** Hold a hand over a warm radiator and the air above it wobbles. The same kind of wobble, on a far larger scale, makes the stars flicker.
