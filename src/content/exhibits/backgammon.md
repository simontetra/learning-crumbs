Backgammon grew out of older race games. A Persian game called nard was played before 800 AD, and it used two dice. The Roman game tabula was one of its ancestors.

Tabula reached Britain in the 1st century AD. In England the game was known as tables, and the name first appears in English print in the *Codex Exoniensis* of 1025.

The doubling cube, which lets a player raise the stakes, is believed to have been introduced in New York in the 1920s. The American rules were revised in 1931.

> **Look closer:** Backgammon is a game of chance as well as skill, because the dice decide every move. That mix is part of why race games like it have lasted so long.
