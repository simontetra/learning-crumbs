Sleep hygiene means the habits that make good sleep more likely. The NHS and Oxford Health suggest these:

- **Keep a regular routine:** go to bed and get up at similar times every day, including weekends.
- **Wind down:** read, listen to calm music or do breathing exercises before bed. Write down worries or tomorrow's tasks to clear your mind.
- **Limit screens:** avoid phones, tablets and computers for an hour before bed, because their blue light can keep you awake.
- **Watch caffeine:** limit tea, coffee and some soft drinks. Oxford Health warns against caffeine after 2pm. The NHS suggests a warm milky drink or herbal tea instead.
- **Go easy on alcohol and nicotine:** avoid them for one to two hours before bed.
- **Keep the bedroom quiet, dark and cool:** earplugs, an eye mask, closed curtains, white noise or an open window can help.
- **Use the bed for sleep:** if you have been awake for about 20 minutes, get up, do something relaxing, and go back to bed when you feel sleepy.
- **Exercise, but not too late:** being active during the day helps, though exercise close to bedtime can keep some people awake.

> **Look closer:** If sleep problems continue despite good habits, the NHS suggests talking to a GP. Oxford Health suggests seeing a GP about insomnia, and mentions a cognitive behavioural therapy programme for it.
