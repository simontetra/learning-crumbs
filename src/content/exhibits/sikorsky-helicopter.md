People dreamed of helicopters long before they could build one. Leonardo da Vinci sketched an "aerial screw" around 500 years ago, and children in ancient China played with spinning bamboo toys that whirled up into the air.

**Igor Sikorsky** was born in **Kiev** in 1889. His mother told him about Leonardo's flying machines, and at the age of **12** he built a little helicopter powered by a rubber band that actually rose into the air.

As a young man he built two real helicopters, in **1909** and **1910**. Neither would fly. So he turned to aeroplanes instead, built some of the world's first large multi-engined aircraft, and in 1919 moved to the United States.

But he never forgot the helicopter. On **14 September 1939**, in Connecticut, Sikorsky lifted off in the **VS-300**, a strange open framework with a pilot's seat. It had **one large main rotor** on top and a **small rotor on the tail** to stop the whole machine spinning round. After much testing it was flying freely by 1940, and in 1941 it set a world record by staying up for over an hour and a half.

Almost every helicopter in the sky today uses that same layout.

> **Look closer:** Like the Wright brothers, Sikorsky started with a rubber-band toy. In 1943 he gave the VS-300 to Henry Ford's museum in Dearborn, Michigan, so people could see where the helicopter began.
