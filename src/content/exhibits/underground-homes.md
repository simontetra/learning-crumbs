Coober Pedy is an opal-mining town in the Australian outback. Summers are very hot, often above 50 degrees Celsius. Miners first sheltered from the heat in old shafts, and living below ground later became the norm. Today more than half the residents live in dugouts, although the town's population is hard to measure because many people travel.

Below ground, the temperature stays at about 25 degrees in summer, and it drops to around minus two in winter. Little heating or cooling is needed.

> **Look closer:** The air above ground swings from very hot to cold, but the ground a short way down stays steady. A home dug into it keeps that steadiness.
