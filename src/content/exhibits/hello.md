When the **telephone** was brand new, nobody knew what to say when they picked it up. There had never been a need for a word like that before.

**Alexander Graham Bell**, who had just invented the telephone, liked **"Ahoy!"**, the call sailors used to hail another ship.

**Thomas Edison** had a different idea. In an **1877** letter he suggested **"Hello"**, because it was loud and clear and easy to hear over a crackly line. Edison didn't invent the word. People already shouted *hallo* or *hullo* to get someone's attention. But the telephone made it the word everyone said.

Hello won. By **1889** the women who connected calls at telephone exchanges were being called **hello girls**.

> **Look closer:** Bell's "ahoy" never quite disappeared. Sailors still use it, and so does Mr Burns in *The Simpsons*.
