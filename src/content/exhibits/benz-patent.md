So when *was* the first car made? It depends what you mean by a car. Cugnot's steam wagon came first, but most people mean something different: a small, light vehicle, driven by its own engine, that ordinary people could use. And for that, there is a date.

On **29 January 1886**, in the German city of **Mannheim**, an engineer called **Karl Benz** applied for a patent for a *Fahrzeug mit Gasmotorenbetrieb*: a **vehicle powered by a gas engine**. It was granted later that year as patent number **37435**.

His **Patent-Motorwagen** had **three wheels**, like a big tricycle, with a small single-cylinder **petrol** engine at the back. Engines like that already existed. Benz's great step was to design a whole vehicle around one, rather than bolting an engine onto a cart. He showed it off in July 1886.

That same year, in another German town, **Gottlieb Daimler** and **Wilhelm Maybach** built a four-wheeled motor carriage, and patented it a few months after Benz. So 1886 was the year the car arrived, from two directions at once. Benz's patent is the one usually called the car's **birth certificate**.

> **Look closer:** Few people believed in the Motorwagen at first. It took Karl's wife, Bertha, and a secret 106-kilometre road trip to show the world what it could do.
