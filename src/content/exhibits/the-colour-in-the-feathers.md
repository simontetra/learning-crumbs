Some dinosaur colours can be worked out, and the method comes from **pigment**. Inside living feathers are tiny structures called melanosomes, which carry the dark pigment melanin. Their shape and size match the colour they produce in living birds.

Researchers found melanosomes in fossil feathers. Sausage-shaped ones point to **black**, and smaller ones to **reddish, rusty** tones. The fossil **Sinosauropteryx**, from about 125 million years ago, seems to have had alternating dark and light bands on its tail, with a russet or gingery shade.

The method has limits. Blue, purple and green sheens are not recovered from these fossils, and other pigments may not have survived. The chemistry has changed over millions of years, so scientists infer colour rather than read it directly.

> **Look closer:** The answer to "what colour were they?" is only partly known. Some dinosaurs were dark or reddish, and a few had stripes. Nobody can yet say whether any were bright blue or green.
