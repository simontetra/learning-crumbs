Lotus leaves look clean even when they grow in muddy water. Their surface does something clever with water.

Looked at under a microscope, the leaf is covered in tiny **bumps**, and each bump has even finer **nanostructures** on top of it. Researchers Wilhelm Barthlott and Christoph Neinhuis described these structures in 1997, after examining lotus leaves with a scanning electron microscope.

Because the surface is so rough at this scale, a water droplet only touches the very top of the bumps. It stays almost round, and it will not settle. Tilt the leaf even slightly, and the drop rolls off, taking any dirt on the surface along with it. Scientists call this the **lotus effect**, and it has inspired self-cleaning paints and fabrics.

> **Look closer:** The leaf doesn't repel water with a coating. Its shape does the work, which is why the effect is still there when the leaf is washed.
