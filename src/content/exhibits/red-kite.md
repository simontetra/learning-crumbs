If you drive along the M40 through the Chiltern Hills, you'll often see large russet birds circling overhead, twisting their **forked tails** to steer. Those are **red kites**, and not long ago you'd have been very lucky to see one anywhere in Britain.

For about 200 years kites were shot, trapped and poisoned, and their eggs were taken by collectors. By the 20th century they had gone from England and Scotland, and only a few pairs clung on in the hills of central Wales.

In **July 1990**, **13** young red kites, recently flown in from the **Navarra** region of Spain, made their first flight in the Chilterns. More releases followed in England and Scotland, while the Welsh birds were protected too.

Thirty years later, there were an estimated **1,800 breeding pairs** across the UK, and more than **10,000** red kites in all. Britain is now home to almost a tenth of all the red kites in the world.

> **Look closer:** In a neat twist, red kites have been declining in Spain. In 2022 British kites were sent to Spain to help, returning the favour of 1990.
