On the dry lake bed of **Racetrack Playa**, in Death Valley, rocks sometimes leave long, straight tracks behind them. For a long time, nobody saw them move.

Then, in 2014, researchers fitted rocks with GPS trackers and set up a camera and a weather station. They found that the rocks move only on a few cold, wet mornings. Overnight, a **thin sheet of ice** forms on the wet mud. In the morning the sun melts it, and the floating panels of ice are pushed by the **wind**. Several rocks can be shoved along at once.

Some rocks weigh around **320 kilograms**, and their tracks can run for **460 metres**. Over one winter, more than sixty rocks were seen moving at speeds of **2 to 5 metres a minute**.

> **Look closer:** The rocks don't need a push from anyone. The ice and the wind do the work, and the trick only works when the conditions are just right.
