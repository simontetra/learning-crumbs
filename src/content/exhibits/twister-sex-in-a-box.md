Twister began in 1965 as an idea for a promotion for a shoe polish brand, Johnson's Wax. Its inventor, Reyn Guyer, worked at his father's design company, and his first test game, called King's Footsie, was turned down by 3M.

The idea was later developed with Chuck Foley and Neil Rabens. At first it was called **Pretzel**, but the name changed to Twister after a rival's toy dog called Pretzel caused a trademark problem. Milton Bradley took the game on, and it reached the shops in 1966.

The first sales meetings went badly. A buyer for the department store Sears called the game too risqué and declined to stock it. Critics agreed that it was too daring, because of how closely players were pressed together, and one called it "sex in a box".

Things changed on 3 May 1966, when Johnny Carson demonstrated the game on *The Tonight Show*. Shoppers asked for it in the shops the next day, and Sears reversed its decision. More than three million sets had sold by 1967.

> **Look closer:** A game that was once thought too daring is now a family favourite, which is a good reminder that tastes change.
