Printers do not print one page at a time. They print several pages on each side of a large sheet, then fold the sheet into sections called **signatures**.

The number of folds sets the size. A sheet folded once is a folio. Folded into quarters, it makes a quarto. Folded again, into eighths, it makes an octavo. Each section is lettered in order, so the book goes together the right way round.

The bookbinder then adds the endpapers and sews the sections together. The book is sewn from the back to the front, on a frame that has hardly changed since the Middle Ages.

> **Look closer:** Every book on our shelf is a pile of folded sheets, sewn in a line. The page you are reading is part of a sheet folded in half, and then in half again.
