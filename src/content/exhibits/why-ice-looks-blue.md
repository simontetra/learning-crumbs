Ice is clear, but a thick block of it can glow blue. The reason is what ice does to light.

White light is made of every colour. Pure ice **absorbs red light**, so red is gradually used up as light passes through. Blue light is not absorbed in the same way, so it passes through and scatters back out. The **longer the light travels through the ice, the bluer it looks**.

Air bubbles change the picture. Ice full of bubbles, such as fresh ice on a pond, scatters light back out before much is absorbed, so it looks white. Thick glacier ice, with fewer bubbles, lets the light travel further, and that is where the blue comes from.

> **Look closer:** The sky is blue for a different reason. Air scatters blue light, while ice absorbs red. Two different processes give the same colour.
