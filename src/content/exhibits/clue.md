In the old Greek story, the hero **Theseus** was sent into the **Labyrinth**, a maze so twisting that nobody who went in ever found their way out, to face the **Minotaur**, half man and half bull.

Princess **Ariadne** gave him a simple, clever gift: a **ball of thread**. He tied one end at the entrance and unwound it as he went deeper. After defeating the Minotaur, he followed the thread all the way back out.

In old English, a ball of thread was called a **clew**. Shakespeare used it in that sense. Because of stories like Theseus's, following a *clew* came to mean following something that leads you, step by step, through a puzzle.

Over time the spelling changed to **clue**, and the meaning moved from the thread itself to any small piece of evidence that helps you find your way to an answer. Every detective story, and every board game of Cluedo, is following a thread.

> **Look closer:** Sailors still use *clew* for the lower corner of a sail. The old word never quite went away.
