The brothers **Joseph-Michel** and **Jacques-Étienne Montgolfier** came from a French family of **paper-makers**. The story goes that Joseph watched smoke and embers rising from a fire and wondered whether whatever lifted them could lift something bigger.

They built bags of cloth and paper and filled them with hot smoke from a fire underneath. They thought the smoke itself contained a special lifting gas, and deliberately burned damp straw and wool to make plenty of it. In fact it was simply the **hot air**, lighter than the cool air around it. They got the right result for the wrong reason.

On **4 June 1783** they flew an unmanned balloon in front of a crowd. Then, on **19 September 1783**, at the Palace of **Versailles** in front of King Louis XVI, a balloon carried the first air passengers: **a sheep, a duck and a rooster**. All three came down safely.

On **21 November 1783**, two men, **Pilâtre de Rozier** and the **Marquis d'Arlandes**, made the first free flight by people, drifting over Paris for about **25 minutes** and some 9 kilometres.

> **Look closer:** Benjamin Franklin watched the flights in Paris. When someone asked what use a balloon was, he is said to have replied, "What use is a newborn baby?"
