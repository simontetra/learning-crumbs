**Mary Anderson** lived in Alabama, where snow is rare. On a winter visit to **New York City**, she rode on a **streetcar** in the snow, and watched the driver struggle. Every so often he had to open his window, or stop altogether, to clear the snow and ice from the glass so he could see.

There had to be a better way. Mary designed one: an arm with a **rubber blade** on the outside of the windscreen, worked by a **lever** from inside. Pull the lever, and the blade swept across the glass and back. It could even be taken off when winter was over. She was granted a patent on **10 November 1903**.

Nobody wanted it. In **1905** a company she wrote to replied that it wasn't worth enough to sell. Cars were still rare, and people couldn't see the point. Her patent ran out in 1920, before wipers caught on, so she never made money from it.

But the idea was right. Within a few years, wipers were becoming standard on cars, and in **2011** Mary Anderson was named in America's National Inventors Hall of Fame.

> **Look closer:** Like Bette Nesmith with her Liquid Paper, Mary Anderson saw a small everyday problem that everyone else had simply put up with.
