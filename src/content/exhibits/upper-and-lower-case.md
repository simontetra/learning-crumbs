In a printing workshop, every letter was a small block of metal. The person who arranged them into words, the **compositor**, worked at a sloping stand holding two shallow wooden trays called **cases**, each divided into little compartments.

The higher case, further from the hand, held the **capital letters**, plus figures and accented letters. Capitals are used less often, so they could sit a little out of the way. The lower case, close at hand, held the **small letters**, punctuation and the blank spaces that go between words, the pieces needed all the time.

So capitals became **upper-case letters** and small letters became **lower-case letters**. The furniture is long gone from most of our lives, but the words are on every keyboard and phone. When a password is "case-sensitive", it is using a term from a printer's workshop.

The compartments in the lower case were not all the same size. Common letters like *e* had big boxes; rare ones like *z* had small ones.

> **Look closer:** Printers left us plenty of other words too, including two that now mean "said too often".
