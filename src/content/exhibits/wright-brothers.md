**Wilbur** and **Orville Wright** were brothers from Dayton, Ohio. In **1892** they opened a **bicycle shop**, selling, mending and later building bicycles. The money from the shop paid for their real passion: working out how to fly.

Bicycles taught them a lot. A bicycle is unstable, but a skilled rider keeps it balanced by constantly steering and leaning. The Wrights believed an aeroplane would be the same: the hard part wasn't just lifting off, it was **control**. They designed ways for the pilot to tilt the wings and steer in the air.

They tested gliders on the windy sand dunes near **Kitty Hawk**, North Carolina, and built their own lightweight engine. On **17 December 1903**, with **Orville** at the controls, the **Wright Flyer** rose into the air and flew for **12 seconds**, covering **120 feet**. That is shorter than the wingspan of a modern jumbo jet.

They made **four flights** that day, taking turns. The longest, with **Wilbur** flying, lasted **59 seconds** and covered **852 feet**. Powered, controlled flight had begun.

> **Look closer:** In 1878, when they were boys, their father brought home a toy helicopter powered by a twisted rubber band. It flew up to the ceiling, and when it broke the brothers built their own. Orville later said that toy started their interest in flight. A very similar toy started Igor Sikorsky on the road to the helicopter.
