In July 1994 Jeff Bezos registered a new company in Washington State to sell books online. He called it **Cadabra, Inc.**, from *abracadabra*, because he wanted shopping on the internet to feel like magic.

The magic did not survive a phone call. When Bezos told his lawyer the name, the lawyer heard *cadaver*, a dead body. A company name that sounds like a corpse over the telephone is a problem, so Bezos went looking for another. He liked "Relentless" for a while too.

He found his answer by reading through the dictionary. **Amazon** was the largest river in the world, which suited a shop that promised to be "Earth's biggest book store". It also began with A. In the early web, many lists of websites were alphabetical, so a name near the start of the alphabet appeared near the top.

The company became Amazon.com a few months after it was founded, and opened for business in 1995.

> **Look closer:** Google's name came from a typing slip. Amazon's came from a mishearing.
