Measure yourself first thing in the morning, then again before bed. You may be a little **shorter** in the evening.

Your **spine** is a stack of bones called **vertebrae**. Between each pair is a squashy cushion called a **disc**. All day long, as you walk, run, sit and stand, the weight of your body presses down on those discs and they slowly flatten. You shrink, just a bit.

At night, lying down, there is no weight pressing on them. The discs soak up fluid and spring back, and by morning you are back to your full height. The exact amount is different for different people.

In **space**, with no gravity squashing the spine at all, the discs spread out even more. Astronauts can grow up to **3 percent taller**, about **5 centimetres** for a tall adult. When they come home, gravity squashes them back down again over the next few months.

> **Look closer:** If you want to look your tallest in a photo, have it taken in the morning.
