In Victorian Britain, the roads belonged to horses. Steam engines on wheels, rumbling and hissing along the lanes, frightened them, and plenty of people wanted them kept firmly in their place.

So in **1865** Parliament passed the **Locomotive Act**, soon nicknamed the **Red Flag Act**. Any road vehicle that wasn't pulled by an animal needed a crew of **three**. One of them had to **walk about 60 yards ahead, carrying a red flag**, to warn people and horses. The speed limit was **4 miles an hour** in the country and **2 miles an hour** in towns, slower than a brisk walk.

In 1878 the flag was dropped and the walker could stay a little closer, but the slow speeds stayed. For early motorists, it was maddening.

Then, in **1896**, a new law raised the limit to **14 miles an hour** and got rid of the walking escort altogether. On **14 November 1896**, drivers celebrated with an **Emancipation Run** from London to Brighton. About 33 cars set off. Roughly 22 made it.

> **Look closer:** The run still happens. Every year, on the first Sunday in November, hundreds of cars built before 1905 chug from London to Brighton in the **Veteran Car Run**.
