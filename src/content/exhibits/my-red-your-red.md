Colour blindness can be tested, because colour-blind people mix up colours that other people can tell apart. But here's a stranger question.

Imagine that when you look at a ripe strawberry, the colour you see is the colour *I* would call green. And when you look at grass, you see what I would call red. But you've always called strawberries **red** and grass **green**, just like me. We'd agree on every name, sort every pencil into the same piles, and stop at the same traffic lights. Would either of us ever find out?

The English philosopher **John Locke** asked a version of this question in **1690**. Philosophers call it the **inverted spectrum**, and they're still arguing about it.

The trouble is that nobody can climb inside anyone else's head. We can measure the light bouncing off a strawberry. We can test which colours someone can tell apart. But the *experience* itself, what the red is like for you, is private. One philosopher has put it simply: there doesn't seem to be any experiment that could settle it.

> **Look closer:** Some of the best questions don't have answers yet. Asking them is how science and philosophy began.
