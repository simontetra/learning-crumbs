About 66 million years ago, an asteroid roughly 10 to 15 kilometres across struck near what is now the Yucatán Peninsula in Mexico. It left a crater about 150 kilometres across. The crater was identified in 1990, and it is now buried beneath the peninsula.

The Smithsonian describes what followed: fires across the world, a tsunami more than half a mile high, acid rain, and dust that blocked sunlight for perhaps a year. Sources put the species losses at between about 70 and 75 per cent, and that includes the non-bird dinosaurs.

> **Look closer:** The birds survived the impact, and they are the living descendants of the dinosaurs. The extinction was not the end of dinosaurs entirely, only of most of the groups that existed then.
