The **bar-tailed godwit** is a long-legged wading bird with a long, slightly upturned beak. Every autumn, godwits that have spent the summer in **Alaska** set off for the other side of the Pacific Ocean.

In **October 2022**, scientists were following one young godwit with a tiny satellite tag, number **234684**. It was only about **five months old**. It had never made the journey before.

It took off from Alaska and flew south over the open Pacific. It passed islands where it could have rested, and kept going. It didn't land. It didn't eat. It didn't stop to sleep. After **11 days and 1 hour**, it came down in **Tasmania**, at the southern tip of Australia, having flown **13,560 kilometres**. It's the longest non-stop flight ever recorded for any bird.

How is it possible? Before they leave, godwits eat until they are very fat, then their bodies shrink some of the organs they won't need in flight. One expert thought the young bird may have lost **half or more of its body weight** on the way.

> **Look closer:** Nobody taught it the way. A five-month-old bird, on its very first trip, crossed the largest ocean on Earth without a single stop.
