In **1960**, glassmakers at Corning in New York started **Project Muscle**, a search for ways to make glass much stronger. They found a clever trick. Dip the glass in a bath of hot, melted salt containing **potassium**, and small **sodium** atoms near the surface swap places with bigger potassium atoms. The bigger atoms squeeze into spaces meant for smaller ones, and the surface of the glass ends up squashed tight, which makes cracks much harder to start.

They called the toughened glass **Chemcor**. It was used in some cars, planes and medicine bottles, but it never really took off, and for years it was more or less forgotten.

Then, in **2005**, Corning began testing whether the glass could be made thin enough for gadgets. Soon after, **Apple** asked Corning for a thin, tough glass for a new phone. After several months of hard work, it was ready for the first **iPhone** in **June 2007**. In 2008 Corning gave it a new name: **Gorilla Glass**.

Today a great many phones, tablets and watches have screens made the same way. An idea that waited on the shelf for forty years turned out to be just right, at last.

> **Look closer:** It is still glass, so it can still crack. But next time a phone survives a fall, think of a 1960s project with a very good name.
