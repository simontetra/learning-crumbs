When the sun is low, its light passes through a much thicker layer of air before it reaches you. The sun appears low in the sky, and the light has a much longer journey.

On that long path, the blue light is scattered most strongly, and it bounces away in other directions several times before it reaches your eyes. More of the yellow and red light survives the trip, so those colours dominate the view.

The same scattering that makes the sky blue at midday leaves the sunset red. It is the same process, with a longer path.

> **Look closer:** Near the horizon the sky often looks whitish or pale. The air there is thicker, so more colours are scattered together, and the blue is less strong.
