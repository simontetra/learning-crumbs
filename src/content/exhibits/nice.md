If you had called someone **nice** in the 1300s, they might not have thanked you.

The word comes from the Latin *nescius*, meaning **not knowing**. When it arrived in English, *nice* meant **foolish** or **silly**. Soon it was being used for all sorts of faults, from showing off to laziness.

Then, slowly, it softened. By the end of the Middle Ages it could mean **shy** or **reserved**, which wasn't really an insult. Later it came to mean **careful** and **precise**, a meaning we still use in phrases like "a nice distinction". By the 1700s, being nice had become a good thing: pleasant, kind, agreeable.

Words drift like this all the time. Nobody decides it; millions of people just use a word a little differently, again and again, until the old meaning fades.

> **Look closer:** So in seven hundred years, *nice* went all the way from an insult to a compliment. Which words do you use that might mean something different in another seven hundred?
