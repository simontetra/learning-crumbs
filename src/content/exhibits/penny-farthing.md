Once pedals were added to bicycles in the 1860s, they were fixed straight onto the front wheel, with no chain and no gears. One turn of the pedals meant one turn of the wheel. So to go faster, makers made the front wheel **bigger**, as big as the rider's legs could reach.

By the **1870s** these **high-wheelers** were popular in England. Riders perched high above the road, and if the wheel hit a stone they could be thrown over the handlebars head first. This was called **"taking a header"**.

At the time they were simply called **"ordinary" bicycles**. The nickname **penny-farthing** came later, from the big penny coin and the tiny farthing coin, which looked like the large and small wheels side by side.

In **1885**, **John Kemp Starley** of Coventry launched the **Rover**. It had two wheels of the same size and a **chain** that drove the back wheel. Because it was so much less likely to throw you off, it was called a **safety bicycle**. Look at almost any bicycle today and you are looking at Starley's design.

> **Look closer:** Cheap safety bicycles gave many women a new freedom to travel on their own. In 1896 the American campaigner Susan B. Anthony said bicycling had "done more to emancipate women than anything else in the world".
