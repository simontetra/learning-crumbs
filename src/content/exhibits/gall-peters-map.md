The Gall–Peters map keeps the area of every country in the right proportion. Africa looks as big as it is, and so does Greenland, which is much smaller.

Its first version came from James Gall, a Scottish clergyman, who described it in 1855 and published it in 1885. It drew little attention. Arno Peters later devised a similar map and presented it in 1973, and the name Gall–Peters came into use after that.

Keeping the area costs something. Shapes are stretched, especially near the equator, so the continents look distorted in a different way from the Mercator.

Many cartographers were not impressed. In 1989 and 1990 seven North American geographic organisations passed a resolution rejecting every rectangular world map, including both the Mercator and the Gall–Peters.

> **Look closer:** No flat map can keep the area, the shape and the distances all at once. Every world map is a compromise, and choosing one is a decision about what to get right.
