Nobody quite invented the skateboard. It grew out of California's surf beaches in the **1950s**. On days when the sea was flat, surfers wanted something to ride on dry land, so they nailed the metal wheels from old **roller skates** onto planks and crates. They called it **"sidewalk surfing"**.

Soon it became a business. In **1957** boards were being sold in Hermosa Beach, and in **1959** a company called Roller Derby made the first **mass-produced** skateboard. Early wheels were metal or clay, which skidded and threw riders off at the smallest stone.

The big change came in **1972**, when a surfer called **Frank Nasworthy** made wheels from **polyurethane**, a tough, grippy plastic. Suddenly boards were faster, quieter and far easier to control.

In **1976** a drought hit California, and many swimming pools were drained. Skaters from a team known as the Zephyr crew began riding up the curved walls of empty pools, inventing the soaring **"vert"** style. Skateboarding has since grown from a beach pastime into an Olympic sport, first held at the Tokyo Games in 2021.

> **Look closer:** Skateboarding borrowed from two older ideas: roller skates for its wheels and surfing for its moves.
