**John Joseph Merlin** was a Belgian inventor living in London, famous for clever clocks, musical instruments and mechanical toys. Around **1760**, the story goes, he arrived at a masked ball wearing boots with small metal wheels set in a line underneath, and glided in while **playing the violin**.

He could neither steer nor stop. He rolled straight into a large and very expensive **mirror**, smashing it, breaking his violin and hurting himself badly. The story was told for years afterwards, and it may have grown in the telling, but Merlin's wheeled boots are often called the first roller skates.

Skates stayed awkward until **1863**, when an American, **James Plimpton**, invented a skate with **four wheels** in two pairs, mounted so they tilted when you leaned. Now skaters could turn simply by shifting their weight.

A craze followed. Roller rinks opened across Europe and America, with orchestras and electric lights. By **1880** London had about 70 rinks, and newspapers mocked the fans as **"rinkomaniacs"**.

> **Look closer:** Merlin's skates had their wheels in a single line, like today's in-line skates. The design came back two centuries later.
