Most sand is made largely of **silica**, the same stuff that makes up most of a window. So why can you see through one and not the other?

Look closely at sand and you will see that it is not one thing but millions of tiny separate **grains**. Every time light reaches the edge of a grain, some of it bounces off in a new direction. With millions of edges, the light gets bounced and scattered so many times that hardly any of it comes straight through. That is why a bucket of sand looks solid and pale. A single clear grain, on its own, lets plenty of light through.

When sand is melted, usually with a little soda to help it melt, the grains flow together into one smooth, continuous piece. There are no more edges inside to scatter the light. And the atoms in glass are not lined up in neat crystal rows, which leaves even fewer places for light to bounce.

There is one more reason, deep inside the atoms. Light can only be soaked up if it brings exactly the right amount of energy to nudge an electron. In glass, ordinary visible light doesn't have enough, so it passes straight through. Ultraviolet light does, so most of it is stopped. That is why it is hard to get a suntan through a window.

> **Look closer:** Smash a piece of clear glass into tiny crumbs and they look white and cloudy, just like sand. All those new edges are scattering the light again.
