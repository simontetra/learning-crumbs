An outline in one of George Orwell's notebooks from 1944 sketches a novel called **The Last Man in Europe**. The title fits the story's hero, Winston Smith, who seems to be the last person left who thinks for himself.

Orwell wrote most of the book in a remote farmhouse on the Scottish island of Jura, while seriously ill with tuberculosis. In October 1948, writing to his publisher Fredric Warburg, he said he still had not decided between *Nineteen Eighty-Four* and *The Last Man in Europe*. Warburg favoured the date, which he thought would sell better.

The novel was published in June 1949 as *Nineteen Eighty-Four*. Orwell died the following January.

Nobody knows for certain why Orwell picked that particular year. A popular theory says he swapped the last two digits of 1948, the year he finished the book, but this has never been confirmed, so treat it as a guess.

> **Look closer:** Orwell finished his final draft by typing it himself, exhausting work for a very sick man. The typewriter has its own gallery.
