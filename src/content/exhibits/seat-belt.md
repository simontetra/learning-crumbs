Early cars had no seat belts at all. Later, some had a simple strap across the lap. It was better than nothing, but in a crash it could do harm of its own.

In **1959**, a Swedish engineer at **Volvo** called **Nils Bohlin** came up with a better design. One strap goes across your **hips**, another goes diagonally across your **chest** and over your **shoulder**, and the two join at a single buckle beside you. The belt is fixed to the car at **three points**. Look at the seat belt in any car today, and you're looking at Nils Bohlin's design.

Then Volvo did something unusual. A company that owns a patent can charge others to use the idea. Volvo chose not to. It let **every other car maker use the design for free**, because it would save lives.

It did. The three-point seat belt is thought to have saved **more than a million lives** around the world.

> **Look closer:** Inventions usually make money for their owners. This one was given away, and that made it matter even more.
