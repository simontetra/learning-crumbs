A duck can sit on a pond all day and stay dry underneath. The secret is in its feathers, and in a small gland near the base of its tail.

Birds rub against the **preen gland**, which is also called the uropygial gland, and collect oil on their beaks. They then spread that oil over their feathers while they preen. This is why birds spend so much time cleaning and smoothing their plumage.

The oil coats the **barbules**, the tiny hooked branches that hold each feather's vane together. Water cannot get past the oil layer, so it beads up and runs off, and the feather stays dry underneath.

> **Look closer:** A feather that is covered in oil and water stays light. Wet feathers, without the oil, lose their shape and can make a bird heavy and cold.
