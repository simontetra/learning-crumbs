Isaac Newton is said to have been sitting in a garden under apple trees, when an apple fell. He began to wonder **why it always fell straight down**, and not sideways or upwards.

We know the story from **William Stukeley**, a friend of Newton's, who wrote it up in his *Memoirs of Sir Isaac Newton's Life*. The book was published in **1752**, about twenty-five years after Newton died, so the details may not be exact. The story does not say that the apple hit his head.

What Newton worked out was that the same pull that brings an apple down also keeps the Moon going round the Earth. Published in **1687**, his theory said that every object pulls on every other object, and that the pull gets weaker the further apart they are.

> **Look closer:** The apple is a good place to start, but the same pull is at work on you, on the chair you are sitting on and on the Moon.
